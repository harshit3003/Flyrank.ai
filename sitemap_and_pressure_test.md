# Portfolio Sitemap & Pressure Test

## 1. The Foundation

**Claim / Proof Statement:**
"I build high-performance, AI-integrated web applications that solve real business problems, proven by my work on scalable dashboards and automated workflows."

**One Action:**
"Book a consultation."

---

## 2. Sitemap Sketch (Single Page Application)

To keep it focused and drive the user to the "One Action", this is a single-page scrolling site.

1. **Hero Section:**
   - Big bold text stating the claim.
   - Subheadline expanding slightly on the value provided.
   - Primary CTA button: "See My Work" (scrolls to Case Studies).
2. **Case Studies Section (The Proof):**
   - 2 or 3 specific projects.
   - Focus on Problem -> Solution (AI integration/performance) -> Impact/Results.
3. **Short About Section:**
   - 2-3 sentences about who I am, my core tech stack, and why I care about solving business problems.
4. **Contact / CTA Section (The Action):**
   - A clear, simple form or direct Calendly link to "Book a consultation."
   - No unnecessary links (like social media icons that distract from the main goal).

---

## 3. Pressure-Test Prompt

**Prompt to AI:**
> "Act as a strict portfolio tutor. My proof statement is: 'I build high-performance, AI-integrated web applications that solve real business problems, proven by my work on scalable dashboards and automated workflows.' My desired one action is for the visitor to 'Book a consultation'. 
> 
> Here is my sitemap: A single page containing: 1. Hero Section stating the claim. 2. Case Studies Section with 2-3 projects showing problem/solution/impact. 3. Short About section. 4. Contact/CTA section to book a consultation. 
> 
> Please pressure-test this sitemap against my one action and claim. Tell me if anything is missing, unnecessary, or poorly placed. What is one thing I should change?"

---

## 4. Pressure-Test Output (Tutor's Feedback)

**Tutor's Output:**
> "Your sitemap is lean and effectively avoids the trap of 'pages just because.' However, after pressure-testing it against your claim ('proven by my work...') and your one action ('Book a consultation'), here is what you need to change:
> 
> **The Change You Must Make:**
> You need a secondary CTA explicitly placed immediately *after* the Case Studies section. 
> 
> **Why?**
> Your claim relies entirely on the 'proof' (the case studies). Once a user reads a compelling case study and believes your claim, their intent to act is at its highest. If you make them scroll through an 'About' section before they can book a consultation, you introduce friction and risk losing them. 
> 
> **Revised Flow:**
> 1. Hero
> 2. Case Studies (The Proof)
> 3. **CTA: 'Book a consultation' (Capture the intent immediately)**
> 4. Short About (For those who need to know the person before buying)
> 5. Final CTA / Contact Form"
